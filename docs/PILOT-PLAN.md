# Pilot plan (4 weeks with the first financier)

**Goal:** find out whether this saves the financier real time and money, and whether they and others would pay for it.

## Before week 1
- Go-live steps done (`GO-LIVE.md`), legal questions answered, Owner has two-step sign-in.
- Write down the **baseline** from the financier: how long the evening entry takes, how many errors or disputes happen in a week, how often a borrower's balance is argued over, how long month-end reports take.
- Agree what is being tested: the daily collection sheet, balances, overdue follow-up, documents, reports.
- Staff accounts created. Show each person only their own part (15 minutes each).

## Weeks 1 and 2: parallel run
- Collectors keep using the paper sheet. Print it from the app each morning (Payments → collection sheet).
- Office enters the day's collections in the app and compares closing balances with the paper sheet.
- Note every difference: who was right, and why.
- Keep a simple log: date, minutes spent on entry, problems, ideas.

## Weeks 3 and 4: app as the main record
- Stop the parallel Excel. The paper sheet is still printed for collectors.
- Use alerts for overdue follow-up and insurance expiry.
- Run month-end reports from the app instead of Excel.

## What to measure
| Measure | How |
|---|---|
| Time for daily entry | minutes per day, vs the baseline |
| Mistakes found | count per week |
| Overdue follow-up | how many overdue loans were chased because of alerts |
| Reports | time to produce the month-end report |
| Staff comfort | each person: easier, same, or harder? and why |
| Would they pay | see below |

## Pricing test (ask the financier, then 5 to 10 others)
Show the collection sheet and the balance view first. Then ask, without leading:
1. What do you use today, and what did it cost? (Many financiers have bought one-time software or a cheap app.)
2. Which of these would you accept? Test one at a time:
   - A. One-time set-up fee, then a small yearly fee
   - B. A small monthly fee per business
   - C. A fee based on the number of active loans
3. Which part would you miss most if it were taken away?
4. What stops you switching?

Do not guess prices here. Record what people say and what they actually agree to pay.

## Feedback to collect every week
- Three things that worked, three that annoyed. Photos of confusing screens.
- Any number that looked wrong (with the loan number and date).
- Anything staff did on paper because the app could not do it.

## Decision at the end of week 4
Continue and invest in the AI assistant, OCR and the borrower website only if most of these are true:
- Balances matched the paper for the last two weeks.
- Daily entry takes less time than before.
- The financier wants to keep using it.
- At least one other financier shows real interest, ideally by agreeing to pay for a pilot.

If not, fix the biggest problem found before building anything new.
